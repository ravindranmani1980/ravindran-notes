Cost hygiene

Billing reports and export, budgets, the monthly clean-up checklist, committed use discounts and Spot VMs.

Google Cloud bills rarely grow because of one big mistake. They grow from many small ones: a VM nobody stopped, disks left behind, reserved IP addresses, logs kept forever, data crossing regions. Cost hygiene is a monthly routine that finds them, plus paying less for what you keep.

See where the money goes

  • Billing reports (Billing > Reports): group by Project, then Service and SKU, then by your costcenter and application labels. Compare the last three months; changes matter more than totals.
  • SKU explains surprises: network egress between regions and to the internet, Cloud NAT data processing, external IP addresses and log ingestion often appear near the top and are easy to miss.
  • Billing export to BigQuery (standard and detailed usage cost, plus pricing) for line-item analysis and showback reports. Turn it on from day one: it isn't retroactive (Guide 3).
  • FinOps hub in the console brings together savings recommendations, commitment utilisation and waste across the billing account.
-- Last month's net cost by project and service (BigQuery billing export)
SELECT
  project.id AS project,
  service.description AS service,
  ROUND(SUM(cost) + SUM(IFNULL((SELECT SUM(c.amount) FROM UNNEST(credits) c), 0)), 2) AS net_cost
FROM `prj-billing-export.billing.gcp_billing_export_v1_012345_6789AB_CDEF01`
WHERE invoice.month = FORMAT_DATE('%Y%m', DATE_SUB(CURRENT_DATE(), INTERVAL 1 MONTH))
GROUP BY project, service
ORDER BY net_cost DESC
LIMIT 25

Set budgets and alerts

  • A budget per project (or per folder or label), with alerts at 50%, 90% and 100% of actual spend and at 100% of forecast. Forecast alerts give you time to act before the month ends.
  • Budgets don't stop spending. They only notify. For sandbox projects, send budget notifications to Pub/Sub and have a Cloud Run function stop VMs or disable billing on the project when the budget is exceeded, knowing that disabling billing shuts services down.
  • Anomaly detection in Billing flags unusual spend spikes by project and service. Check that the right people get the emails.
# Budget of $500 a month on one project, with alerts at 50%, 90%, 100% and forecast 100%
gcloud billing budgets create --billing-account=012345-6789AB-CDEF01 --display-name="prj-notes-test monthly" `
    --budget-amount=500USD --filter-projects=projects/prj-notes-test `
    --threshold-rule=percent=0.5 --threshold-rule=percent=0.9 --threshold-rule=percent=1.0 `
    --threshold-rule=percent=1.0,basis=forecasted-spend

The monthly clean-up checklist

Find-GcpOrphanedResources.ps1 covers most of this list across every project:

Look forWhy it costs
Unattached persistent disksCharged per GB-month whether attached or not
Old snapshots and custom images nobody usesCharged per GB stored
Reserved external IP addresses not in useUnused static IPs are charged at a higher rate than ones in use
VMs stopped for weeksNo compute charge, but disks and reserved IPs still bill; snapshot and delete
Load balancer backend services with no backendsForwarding rules are charged hourly, doing nothing
Idle Cloud SQL instancesStop non-production instances out of hours (storage still bills)
Log buckets with long retention, and verbose logsIngestion and storage (Guide 9)
Forgotten projectsOld proofs of concept with running resources; the unattended project recommender finds them

Recommender (Active Assist) does much of the finding for you: idle VMs, idle persistent disks, idle IP addresses, idle Cloud SQL instances, machine type rightsizing and unattended projects, each with an estimated saving. Review them in the FinOps hub or the Recommendation Hub monthly.

# Idle disk recommendations for one zone
gcloud recommender recommendations list --project=prj-notes-test --location=us-east1-b `
    --recommender=google.compute.disk.IdleResourceRecommender `
    --format="table(description, primaryImpact.costProjection.cost.units)"

Pay less for what you keep

OptionHow it worksUse for
Sustained use discountsAutomatic, for eligible machine series running much of the monthNothing to do; know which series get them
Resource-based committed use discountsCommit to vCPUs, memory, GPUs or local SSD in a region for 1 or 3 yearsSteady, predictable VM fleets that won't change region
Compute flexible CUDs (spend-based)Commit to an hourly spend for 1 or 3 years across Compute Engine, GKE and Cloud Run, any region and machine familySteady spend that moves between services and regions
Spend-based CUDs for other servicesCommit to spend on Cloud SQL, Memorystore, Cloud Run and othersDatabases and managed services you'll keep
Spot VMsMuch cheaper, but can be stopped at any timeBatch, CI, stateless and fault-tolerant work
  • Rightsize first, then commit. A commitment on an oversized fleet locks in the waste.
  • Commit to the floor, not the average: cover the usage that's there at 3 a.m. on a Sunday, and let on-demand and Spot handle the rest. The CUD recommendations in the console do the maths.
  • Share commitments across the billing account (CUD sharing), so a discount bought in one project covers matching use in others, and track utilisation monthly.
  • Google changed how spend-based CUDs appear on the bill (discounts applied to the price instead of credits). If you have reports built on the billing export, check them against Google's data model notes for the change.

Storage and network

  • Cloud Storage Autoclass moves objects between Standard, Nearline, Coldline and Archive by access pattern; lifecycle rules do it on a schedule for data you know is cold, and delete old versions.
  • Egress is charged when data leaves a zone, region or Google's network. Keep chatty services in one region, use Private Google Access and Cloud CDN, and watch cross-region replication you didn't mean to set up.
  • Cloud NAT charges per gigabyte processed; traffic to Google APIs should use Private Google Access instead of going through NAT.
  • Persistent disk type: balanced rather than SSD where latency allows; don't over-provision Hyperdisk IOPS.

Make it routine

  • A monthly cost review with the owners of the top ten cost items: what changed, what can go, what to commit to.
  • Stop schedules for non-production (Invoke-GcpVMPowerSchedule.ps1, or native instance schedules) and budgets on every sandbox project.
  • Label compliance as a standing item: unlabelled cost is cost nobody owns (Get-GcpUnlabeledResources.ps1).
  • Show the savings. A monthly note on what was removed and what it saved keeps the routine funded.
